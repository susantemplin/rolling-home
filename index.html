🗺️ Pet food banks PetSmart near me 🐕 Dog parks
Pets in the van:
  • A closed van gets dangerous fast in the heat, even with windows cracked. In summer, your pet goes where you go.
  • Summer pavement can burn a dog's paws. If it's too hot for the back of your hand, it's too hot for them.
  • Keep a collar or tag with your number on it, and a leash or carrier ready.
  • Always leave water out. Tuck a litter box where it can't spill when you drive, and pick up after a dog every time.

My van

Where to park

The kinds of places people sleep in their vans around the valley, with the rules for each. Rules change, so read posted signs wherever you stop.

National forest land

The Tonto National Forest borders the valley to the northeast. Many areas allow free camping outside of campgrounds, usually up to 14 days in one spot. Fire restrictions are common, so check before you light anything.

🗺️ Find on map Forest rules

BLM public land

Bureau of Land Management land is often free to camp on, generally up to 14 days within any 28 days. It's usually farther out and more rugged, so arrive in daylight.

🗺️ Find on map BLM Arizona

Store parking lots

Some Walmarts and other big stores allow one night, but it's up to each store and each city. Always ask a manager first. Arrive late, leave early, and don't set anything up outside.

🗺️ Stores near me

Truck stops

Love's, Pilot and Flying J are often okay for a night. Park in the car or RV area, never in truck spots, and buy something inside. Most have paid showers.

🗺️ Truck stops near me

City streets

Each city in the valley has its own rules about sleeping in a vehicle, and some areas ban it. Avoid neighborhoods, don't stay on the same street two nights in a row, and move on if anyone asks.

Quiet hours and noise

Every valley city has its own noise rules, and the exact hours differ. A safe habit anywhere is to treat about 10 pm to 7 am as quiet time.

  • No music, slamming doors or engine running at night.
  • Run a generator only in daytime, and never in town.
  • In store lots, the store's own rules come first. Ask.
  • On forest and BLM land, follow posted quiet hours and fire rules.
Look up a city's noise rules
Stay safe:
  • Park facing out so you can leave fast.
  • Keep your keys in the same spot every night.
  • Crack a window for air.
  • Never leave a pet in a closed van when it's warm. It gets dangerous fast.
  • Check in with someone you trust every day from the Today tab.

Van life tips

  • Window covers keep the van cooler and keep people from looking in. Reflective ones help most in summer.
  • Look like you're just parked. No chairs, no clotheslines, no cooking outside in town.
  • Scout spots in daylight, then come back after dark.
  • Freeze water bottles at a friend's or buy ice. A cooler with ice is your fridge.
  • Keep the gas tank at least a quarter full so you can always leave or run the AC.
  • Baby wipes and dry shampoo stretch the days between showers.
  • A small battery pack charged at the library keeps your phone alive overnight.
  • One bag, one spot. Give everything a home so the van doesn't get cluttered.
